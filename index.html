05 / BEFORE YOU DROP IN

Good
questions.

It’s an early preview. Here’s exactly what you’re getting.

Is ClipVault free? Do I need an account?

The 0.3.1 desktop preview is free to download. No account is needed for local recording, and no footage is uploaded automatically. There’s no payment or subscription in this release.

Can I record at 1440p and 120 FPS?

Yes. Version 0.3.1 adds 1440p and 120 FPS options, plus a Quality preset at 80 Mbps. Bitrate starts at 25 Mbps and goes up to 120 Mbps; the Quality preset uses 80 Mbps. Eligible NVIDIA captures now keep native-size frames on the GPU, with compatibility fallback. This path still needs testing on the RTX 5060 Ti. Audio uses continuous sample timing and larger ordered batches to reduce choppiness. Smaller sources keep their aspect ratio without upscaling. Sustained 120 FPS depends on your display, game and encoder headroom; check the live capture FPS. A real FFmpeg test preserved distinct 1440p/120 FPS motion and continuous audio through recording and MP4 export. This is not a Fortnite performance benchmark.

Can I choose which screen gets recorded?

Yes. Open Recorder settings → Screen to record, pick your named monitor or Follow Windows primary screen, then Apply settings while stopped. Start the buffer afterward. Recording stops if the selected display disconnects. Multiple-monitor placement has logic tests; a physical two-monitor check remains pending.

Does the Xbox Share button work?

There’s a dedicated Windows GameInput path for Share/Clip and Guide, alongside standard Xbox button mapping. API availability was checked, but physical Share-button tests are still pending. Firmware, Windows, and USB/Bluetooth connections can affect support. Game Bar may also respond to the button.

Will it affect my Fortnite gameplay?

Recording uses CPU, GPU, and disk resources. Start with the Gaming preset: 720p, 30 FPS, 25 Mbps. Fortnite frame times and FPS have not been benchmarked against Medal. Hardware encoding availability depends on your PC; CPU fallback is included. Heavy load can stop recording if audio falls behind.

What has actually been tested?

84 logic and API tests pass across the project. Installer checks, engine download and offline reuse, saved monitor preference, and an installed Windows ARM64 15-second MP4 save/decode have been exercised. Recording continued during saving. The audio pipeline passed a prior 0.3.0 installed-app check within 104 ms. The 0.3.1 save/decode succeeded, but fresh synchronization testing was inconclusive because the expected pattern could not be isolated. Device-specific timing, native x64 gaming, and physical controller testing still need wider checks.

Can I trim, upload, or share a clip link in the desktop app?

This downloadable preview saves MP4 files locally. Upload those files manually to a service you choose. Desktop login, one-click upload, built-in trimming, automatic game detection, and a hosted ClipVault clip-sharing platform aren’t included in this download.

Where are my files, and how do I uninstall?

Clips save to Videos/ClipVault by default, or your selected output folder. Use Open clips folder in the app. Closing the window keeps ClipVault in the tray; choose Quit ClipVault to exit. Remove it through Windows Installed apps. Your clips and preferences are preserved.
